Radius ranges above describe requested primary radii (including both ends of variable
sweepers and both S-curve bends); JSON also retains measured window minimum radii,
all curvature percentiles, per-family exposure, topology, and validation status.
Primary coverage includes independently requested straight maneuvers. Active exposure
integrates nonzero-curvature source segments, including supporting curves, separately.
Supporting fractions above 40% occur only in specialists, whose Phase 5.1 floor stays
50%. Composites enforce primary >=60%, supporting <=40%, meaningful per-family
exposure, and unchanged geometry/clearance limits.

Compared with Phase 5.1:

- The 245 m composite's supporting share falls **59.1% → 37.7%** at 210 m;
  active exposure rises **25.7% → 41.3%**, significant turns 9 → 15, and
  primary family changes 8 → 11. The long composite improves primary coverage
  55.6% → 64.2%, active exposure 41.3% → 52.6%, and turns 13 → 23 at 330 m
  versus 270 m. Added opposing maneuvers provide useful content rather than
  relabeling closure gaps as primary straights.
- The length-matched 170 m sweeper improves active exposure 59.6% → 72.6%,
  turns 5 → 9 and curvature reversals/100 m 1.18 → 3.53. The second sweeper
  uses a different signed order, variable radii and no supporting turns; it has
  more turns but worse periodic-curvature repetition (.451 versus .000).
- The length-matched S-curve has 8 → 10 windows, 21 → 27 significant turns and
  78.4% → 91.2% active exposure. Chicane practice is broadly unchanged and its
  primary coverage declines slightly. Both retain ring-like scaffolds.
- Only the original folded hairpin strategy survives. Two-bank and orthogonal
  clusters failed initially; outward three-/four-cluster alternatives also
  self-intersected in the adjustment pass. No alternative hairpin topology is
  claimed as accepted. The compact mixed composite could not close within its
  fixed content/length budget; its coverage constraints were not weakened.

These are useful improvements in composites, medium sweepers and S-curves, with
**incomplete hairpin topology diversification**. Different lap lengths, seven
survivors and rejected candidates prevent an overall controlled superiority claim.
The six strategy names describe construction choices, not screened independence.


The default remains V1. The draft
[v2_design_contract.json](../generated_maps/phase5_2_pilot/v2_design_contract.json)
loads through `load_dataset_spec()` and records the unchanged 20 specialist / 20
composite training, 8 validation and 12 testing allocations and scale quotas.
V2 lineages have a version namespace. Candidate seeds remain deterministic;
attempts rotate through three strategies, so failures can fall back to an existing
arrangement. Native recipes record `design_version`, resolved inventory, requested
radii/angles, topology, limits, seed, lineage and solver budget. Metadata retains
solved connector lengths/angles. V1 omits the new native key to preserve its exact
configuration identities and replay bytes.

V2 specialists retain their explicit 50% dominance/primary threshold; V2 composites
always enforce at least 60% primary / at most 40% supporting, even if a caller supplies
weaker composite objectives. Safety limits and the frozen independence policy remain
unchanged. The allocation and recipe mechanism is prepared; **full 60-slot geometric
feasibility and independence are unproven**, especially the rejected hairpin variants.
This pilot does not establish V2 readiness or authorize a release.
